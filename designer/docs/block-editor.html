One box, declarative relations

The editor has no actuator-type menu. Start with one box and change its dimensions, relations and side connections. A block is a statement about the same design. Its position in the list does not change its meaning.

Use the editor

  • Drag a description phrase from the palette to “A box actuator…” or the 3D view. Clicking its add button also works.
  • Drag a circular dimension handle in the full-open 3D view. Linked dimensions update together. With an editable ratio block, dragging the rear dimension edits that ratio; fixing the ratio preserves it instead.
  • Ctrl-click a visible side, tap its −/+ control, or select a side and use the action button to remove/restore its folding panels. A direct side connection is the resulting topology.
  • Use ○ Fix beside a dimension to preserve its value. The fixed-value block records the constraint.
  • Remove a block with ×. Removing a shape clause restores its neutral effect: a rectangular outline, zero front/rear tilt, equal left/right heights, or a restored folding wall. Other explicit conditions and fixed dimensions remain in force. Removing a fixed-value block unlocks its dimension without resetting its number.
  • Reorder relation cards by dragging their text area. The result is order-independent. Inputs and delete buttons are excluded from dragging.
  • Fold is a preview of the same design. Shape handles appear at full opening. Editing geometry returns to the full-open design pose.
  • Use Fit view after large changes. Rotation, zoom and pan are separate from the design and do not change geometry.

Independent and dependent values

The initial equal-width and equal-rise relations are defaults. Explicit user relations replace defaults in the same family. A direct connection can supersede the initial equal-rise assumption; a user-authored required rise ratio is preserved and can conflict instead. Superseded defaults are reported as inactive by the API and omitted from the description cards.

Conflicts and ranges

Conflicts return source block IDs, implicated fields and available condition-removal actions. Blocks and fields are highlighted. The previous valid model stays visible and is labelled as such; saving and exporting are disabled until the draft resolves. Undo/redo includes constraint edits and direct manipulation.

For example: link rear width to 0.8 × front width, remove the left wall, then explicitly request rear rise = 0.5 × front rise. The width relation, rise relation and side connection are identified as incompatible. Removing the rise condition allows rear rise to be calculated at 0.8 × front rise.

Range queries probe the connected feasible construction branch around the resolved input and refine detected boundaries. They include current solver limits (such as minimum dimensions), panel insets and folding-layout overlap. They are numerical local bounds, not exact global interval proofs. An open upper bound means that no upper boundary was found within the search. Thickness motion clearance, continuous collisions, end films and print fit are separate checks.

Linear width/rise/tilt relations and fixed dimensions are solved simultaneously, retaining source IDs through elimination. For example, a fixed rise plus a rise ratio and tilt can determine length. Nonlinear geometric feasibility is checked by the box provider after this solve; this is not a general global nonlinear optimizer. A range query holds the other free driving values at their current values rather than silently resizing unrelated parts.

Language input

This is an explicit, controlled-language adapter, not a general-purpose language model. Unknown statements reject the entire text transaction. Numerical dimension statements become explicit fixed-value constraints so that later statements do not silently overwrite them. Every generated condition retains its originating clause.

length 75 mm;
front width 60 mm;
rear width 0.8 times front width;
remove left wall

Other examples: equal widths, equal rises, rear rise 0.5 times front rise, tilt -10 deg, restore left wall, lock front width. A future natural-language interpreter can produce the same intent document while using the same constraint service and kernel.

Selections and edge-midpoint handles

Click a block, focus one of its controls, or press Enter on its card to highlight its plate regions and edges. Front, Rear, Left and Right labels stay attached to the base reference frame while orbiting and folding. Front/rear rise handles sit at the centres of the front/rear top-panel edges; left/right handles sit at the centres of the side edges. The interaction API supplies the current posed anchors, including mirrored direct-hinge cases.

A rise-handle drag targets the height of that physical edge midpoint. The constraint service converts that geometric goal to existing intent edits and preserves fixed dimensions and explicit relations. Other linked heights may change. “Front max. rise” and “Rear max. rise” in the dimension panel still denote maximum hinge-axis edge heights; they need not equal midpoint heights on a laterally inclined top. The lateral block ratio continues to refer to the front hinge-axis corners, as stated on the block. The two side-height inputs show side-edge midpoint heights.

Selection colours and label placement belong to the UI. The kernel returns neutral regions, edge geometry and anchors through design.handles; intent.command accepts an edge-rise geometric editing goal.

Pick geometry to edit a block

Click inside a top or bottom plate to select the outline block. Click a front/rear top edge to select the front/rear height relation; click a side edge or folding-wall panel to select the lateral relation. A direct-hinge side selects the fixed-side connection. A compact editor below the 3D view exposes the selected edge height, ratio, angle or fixed side.

For a property without an explicit block, selection shows a descriptive view card without changing the saved intent. The first value edit creates the corresponding condition. Panning and orbiting do not select blocks. Clicking and keyboard selection use the same mapping.

Removing a tilt clause restores a zero-angle baseline, including when its input was a height ratio. Removing an outline clause restores equal widths; removing an extra lateral ratio restores equal side profiles. Restoration is scoped to that feature and is not a rewind of unrelated edits. Other retained constraints can impose dependent geometry, with the cause reported. Fixed values are preserved.

Display and drag recovery

The Display menu at the top-right of the 3D view independently controls direction labels, dimension labels, selection highlights, height guides and handles. These are browser-local preferences, not actuator parameters. Label offsets remain stable while orbiting; Reset label layout explicitly recomputes their initial arrangement. Rendering is scheduled once per animation frame.

A 3D drag starts from the exact resolved design shown in the viewport. Each sample is evaluated against that immutable gesture origin. Rejected samples leave the displayed design and its dimensions intact and do not become inputs to a later gesture. This differs from a manually edited conflicting draft, which remains available for deliberate correction. One gesture forms one Undo step.

Paper fit, rigid folding, the sampled assembly path and panel clearance are reported separately. Exceeding A4 does not make the actuator geometry invalid or prevent further resizing; print/export controls retain their paper-fit checks to avoid cropped templates.

Save writes origami-box/project/1 with the full intent, block provenance, fixed values, algorithm version and generated design. Opening checks that the intent reproduces the geometry. Raw design specs and supported older design artifacts can still be imported through explicit adapters.

Describe the actuator

The four basic clauses are “with a trapezoidal outline”, “with unequal front/rear lift”, “with unequal left/right lift”, and “opening about its left/right edge”. They modify one box. Angle and height ratio are alternative inputs to one longitudinal clause. Switching input mode preserves the resolved geometry and block position. Editing a basic clause replaces its previous relation; explicit simultaneous constraints remain available through the API.

Left/right height ratio is measured at the front top hinge-axis edge. Front/rear dimensions denote maximum edge heights. Two front heights and the longitudinal relation determine the other heights of the congruent rigid top. A directly connected edge has zero lift and conflicts with a finite left/right height ratio. A pinned lateral ratio makes a side-height drag adjust the other side too. Neutral baselines yield to geometry imposed by a direct side connection; the editor reports this dependency. Conflicting fixed dimensions are reported instead of being silently changed.
